# Retención en YouTube: hook, intro y estructura para documentales de historia del fútbol (5-12 min)

Context: Spanish-language channel "LOS 11". Videos are narrated with an AI voice over still photos plus some clips. First video: Liverpool vs Milan, 2005 Champions League final in Istanbul. Research was done in November 2026.

Source quality: YouTube Help pages are primary and authoritative, but they give definitions and rules, not best practices. The "How to Succeed in MrBeast Production" memo (leaked in 2024, possibly written around 2022) is semi-primary. Two former producers reportedly confirmed it is genuine; it has not been officially confirmed. Most numeric "rules" (70% at 30 seconds, re-hooks every N seconds) come from creator-tool vendor blogs (vidIQ, OutlierKit, Subscribr and others). They are rules of thumb, not published YouTube data.

## 1. Hook (first 5-30 seconds): what works

### Takeaway
Open on the most dramatic moment or the central contradiction of the story, with no greeting and no "in this video". Within about 10 seconds, prove the promise in the title and thumbnail. Then state the stakes and leave an open question that only the ending answers. YouTube itself measures the opening as the percentage still watching after 30 seconds, and links a high value to the opening matching the title and thumbnail.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube's "Key moments for audience retention" report includes an **Intro** moment: "Intro tells you what percentage of your audience still watched your video after the first 30 seconds." A high percentage suggests the opening matched the title and thumbnail and kept viewers interested. The report needs a video of at least 60 seconds with at least 100 views. — [YouTube Help: Measure key moments for audience retention](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- The MrBeast memo says: "The first minute of each video is the most important minute of each video." In one example, 21M of 60M viewers who clicked had left by minute 1. The first minute must confirm the promise of the thumbnail and title; otherwise viewers "feel like you were lied to and click off". — [MrBeast production memo (transcribed by Alexander Jarvis)](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/); [Passionfruit on the document's authenticity](https://passionfru.it/mrbeast-leaked-document-81151/)
- The memo also says "Stop telling people what they will be watching and start showing them". That is, show rather than announce. — [Alexander Jarvis / MrBeast memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- OutlierKit (September 2026) proposes a 30-second hook in three blocks:
  - **0-10 s:** prove the title with a concrete example, with no greeting or name.
  - **10-20 s:** why it matters to the viewer.
  - **20-30 s:** one sentence on what the video covers.
  - Hold backstory until about minute 3.
  - Things to cut: greetings, backstory before the promise, detailed tables of contents.
  — [OutlierKit: hooks and retention](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- Spanish-language guides agree: open with the result or the promise, avoid "bienvenidos a mi canal" ("welcome to my channel") and the summary of what will be covered, and consider starting "a few seconds into the action" (in medias res). — [Gyre (ES)](https://gyre.pro/es/blog/how-to-improve-audience-retention-on-youtube-top-tips); [Socialkit (ES): hook formulas](https://socialk.it/es/blog/youtube-video-hook-formulas)
- A vidIQ video says a dip in the first few seconds "usually comes down to not hooking viewers quickly enough". — [vidIQ via Rosetta](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/audience-retention-dreadful-try-this)
- In the vidIQ summary of Paddy Galloway, his retention framework is described as adapted from the 5-act structure: hook, rising action, climax. This is a secondary source; I did not find his own material. — [vidIQ / Rosetta: "YouTube Expert Reveals His Viral Video Formula"](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/youtube-expert-reveals-his-viral-video-formula?t=396)

### Inferences
- **Hook rule for Istanbul 2005 (suggested script, not tested).** Use a cold open in medias res at half-time, at 3-0 to Milan, and use the contrast as the open loop. For example: "Descanso en Estambul. Milan 3, Liverpool 0. Nadie en la historia había remontado algo así en una final..." ("Half-time in Istanbul. Milan 3, Liverpool 0. No one in history had ever come back from that in a final...") Close the first 30 seconds with an implicit promise of how it happened, without giving away the result if you can avoid it. Since the result is famous, the loop can be the *how*: "six minutes of madness", Dudek, Shevchenko. Check the facts and the minutes against the sources from the other research areas.
- In a photo-based video, the hook needs the strongest image or clip (the score at half-time, faces in the stands) in the first 3-5 seconds, matching the thumbnail exactly.
- Avoid "Hola a todos, hoy en LOS 11 vamos a ver..." ("Hi everyone, today on LOS 11 we're going to look at..."). It goes against every source consulted.

### Gaps
- **Football channel examples.** I could not find (within the search budget) verifiable analyses or transcripts of the openings used by Tifo Football, Footballers' Stories, Ultimate Football, or the Spanish-language narrators "Relatos de fútbol", "Fútbol de Primera" or "Rincón del fútbol". Searches returned only generic descriptions: Tifo describes itself as "in-depth tactical, historical and geopolitical breakdowns" ([CapCut guide](https://www.capcut.com/create/football-content-pillars-channel)). Watching 5-10 of their openings directly and timing hook versus intro is recommended.
- There is an academic paper on Spanish-language football infotainment YouTubers (UPC, Peru) that I did not review. — [UPC repository](https://repositorioacademico.upc.edu.pe/handle/10757/673878)
- The "63 hooks tested" article (socialvideoplaza) could not be read directly. Only a summary from search was available.

## 2. Intro and branded animation: should it exist, how long, where

### Takeaway
There is no rigorous study, but every source points the same way. Either drop the branded intro or keep it to 2-5 seconds maximum, and always put it **after** the hook, never at second 0. The goal is to get into the content within 10-20 seconds.

### Cited Findings
- A vidIQ video shows retention graphs where a **2-second** branded intro caused a drop of about **4% in 4 seconds**. The presenter calls intros over 5 seconds "too long" and argues that even 2 seconds can hurt. Evidence comes from a single creator's channels. — [vidIQ via Rosetta: "Stop making this huge YouTube mistake"](https://rosetta.to/u/vidiq/stop-making-this-huge-youtube-mistake)
- Dexxter Clark analysed **63 retention graphs** from his own channels. Most of the drop happens during the intro and eases once the intro ends. He recommends reaching the content in about **10-20 seconds**, and the highest 30-second retention (70%+) came from openings that were "barely intros". This is from a search summary; the full article was not read. — [Social Video Plaza](https://www.socialvideoplaza.com/en/articles/what-to-say-youtube-intro-hook)
- Teleprompter.com gives a general intro of 20-30 seconds for most content and 10-15 seconds as the "gold standard" for top channels. "Intro" here means the whole opening segment, not the animated logo. — [Teleprompter: how long should a YouTube intro be](https://www.teleprompter.com/blog/how-long-should-a-youtube-intro-be)

### Inferences
- **Practical rule.** Hook (15-30 s), then a logo or title card of 2-3 s ("LOS 11" over the match title, with a sound stinger), then straight into act 1. Alternatively, no animation at all and just a small watermark or a text overlay with the name.
- Measure it: compare the drop in the curve in the seconds where the card appears against the channel's videos without one.

### Gaps
- No data from YouTube or controlled A/B tests on branded intros. The evidence is anecdotal from single creators.

## 3. Script structure and pacing for 5-12 minutes

### Takeaway
- **Structure:** hook → setup → escalation → climax → quick resolution.
- **Re-engagement:** about every 2-3 minutes, and/or at 40-50% of the video.
- **Climax:** around 2/3 of the way through, not in the final seconds.
- **Visual change:** every 5-10 seconds for faceless or photo-based videos.
- **Ending:** abrupt, with no announcing "we're wrapping up".

### Cited Findings
- **MrBeast memo timeline (about 10-14 minute videos; average 818 s across 100 videos):**
  - **Minute 1:** hook and promise.
  - **Minutes 1-3:** show progress quickly. Include a "3 minute re-engagement" around minute 3.
  - **Minutes 3-6:** "the next most important part"; put the most exciting and simple content here.
  - **Around minute 6:** another re-engagement.
  - **The weaker content** goes in the second half.
  - The memo also says: "If we can get them to watch the first half of the video there's a very high chance they'll watch to the end."
  — [Alexander Jarvis / MrBeast memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- **OutlierKit (2026):**
  - Tease the next segment every **2-3 minutes**, before it starts; most mid-video drop-off happens at transitions.
  - Put the best moment at about **2/3** of the video, not at the end.
  - For faceless videos, change something on screen every **5-10 s** (new map, zoom, caption, cut).
  - A still image held for about **30 s** is a point where viewers leave.
  - A cited example has a "surprise" every **20-30 s**.
  — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Conflicting re-hook cadence:**
  - Another faceless-channel guide recommends re-hooking every **30-60 s** with questions, visual changes and open threads.
  - Another recommends a mid-video re-hook at **40-50%** of the video.
  - A glossary claims every **10-15 s**.
  These are all vendor blogs with no data. — [Overseeros: retention loops](https://www.overseeros.com/blog/youtube-retention-loops); [Tubemilestone](https://tubemilestone.com/blog/longer-youtube-videos-retention.html); [Socialync glossary](https://www.socialync.io/glossary/hook)
- **Spanish-language editing advice:** J and L cuts, varied b-roll, a clear structure. — [Socialkit ES retention guide](https://socialk.it/es/blog/youtube-audience-retention-guide)
- **YouTube's view on peaks and dips:**
  - Spikes can mean engagement, or confusing content being rewatched.
  - Dips mean viewers are leaving or skipping.
  - If the "Top moments" come late, YouTube suggests bringing compelling content forward, because the audience shrinks over the video.
  — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- **Official chapter requirements:**
  - The first timestamp must be **00:00**.
  - At least **3** timestamps, in ascending order.
  - Each chapter must last at least **10 s**.
  - Chapters are added in the description; manual chapters override automatic ones.
  - Automatic chapters are on by default.
  — [YouTube Help: video chapters](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579)
- **Ending, per MrBeast:** "Don't ever signal the end of the video unless it's to build hype for the prize or payoff"; "The video endings must always be abrupt to protect retention." — [Alexander Jarvis / MrBeast memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)
- **Ending, per OutlierKit:** avoid saving the best moment for the final 30 s. — [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Official end screen rules:** the video must last at least **25 s**; the end screen can occupy the last **5-20 s**; up to **4 elements** in 16:9. — [YouTube Help: add end screens](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6388789)

### Inferences (draft structure for an 8-10 minute Istanbul 2005 video)

| Time | Block | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:25 | Cold open | Half-time at 3-0, the stakes, the open question |
| 0:25-0:28 | Card | "LOS 11" (optional) |
| 0:28-2:00 | Act 1, context | Each team's road to the final, the favourite Milan versus an underdog Liverpool. Close with a tease: "Pero nada de eso preparó a nadie para lo que pasó en el minuto 1..." ("But none of that prepared anyone for what happened in the first minute...") |
| 2:00-3:30 | Act 2a, the collapse | First half, 0-3. Re-hook around 3:00: what happened in the dressing room. |
| 3:30-6:30 | Act 2b, the comeback | Second half, the three goals in a short spell. Main climax at about 60-70% of the video. |
| 6:30-8:30 | Act 3 | Extra time, Dudek's double save, the penalties. Second climax and payoff of the opening loop. |
| 8:30-9:00 | Quick resolution | One line of legacy, then a direct cut to the end screen (last 5-20 s) suggesting the next video. No long outro. |

Further rules for this structure:
- Put chapters at 0:00 and at each act change (at least 3, each of 10 s or more). Chapter titles should be descriptive but should not spoil the open loops.
- **Photo-based visuals.** With still photos and an AI voice, slow visual pacing is the main risk. Move each photo (Ken Burns effect, zoom, pan) and change the image or graphic every 5-10 s. Insert score graphics, maps, line-ups or real clips as pattern interrupts. Never leave a static image on screen for 20-30 s.
- **Voice.** The AI voice should vary its rhythm: pauses before goals and short sentences at the climaxes. This is my own inference; I found no data on AI voices and retention.

### Gaps
- No empirical study compares re-hook frequencies. The sources contradict each other (10-15 s, 30-60 s, 2-3 min, 40-50%).
- No source analyses the effect of chapters on retention. Chapters let viewers skip, which may lower average view duration (AVD) but improve satisfaction; unverified.
- No data on retention with AI voices in Spanish.

## 4. Retention benchmarks

### Takeaway
YouTube publishes no official "good" figure. It recommends comparing against the channel's own "typical retention" (the last 10 videos of similar length). As a rule of thumb, about 70% at 30 seconds is considered strong, and under 50% at 30 seconds signals a weak hook.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube offers "typical retention" to compare against your **10 most recent videos of similar length**. — [YouTube Help 9314415](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314415)
- **30-second rules of thumb:**
  - Top videos retain **70%+** in the first 30 s; under **50%** suggests the hook needs work.
  - It is common to lose **20-40%** in the first 15-30 s.
  These are vendor sources with no published method. — [Subscribr](https://subscribr.ai/p/youtube-analytics-improve-video-hooks-intros); [vidIQ: audience retention](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/audience-retention-secrets-youtube/); [1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/how-to-hook-viewers-in-the-first-30-seconds-of-a-youtube-video)
- **Average overall retention:** an average of about **23.7%** is cited, with only **16.8%** of videos above 50%. The original report could not be verified, so treat these figures with caution. — [TubeAnalytics (ES)](https://tubeanalytics.net/es/blog/what-is-a-good-youtube-retention-rate)
- **Length affects the comparison:** long videos have a lower retention percentage. A 25-minute documentary at 42% can generate more watch time than a 6-minute tutorial at 55%, so compare like with like. — [TubeAnalytics (ES)](https://tubeanalytics.net/es/blog/what-is-a-good-youtube-retention-rate); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-hooks-and-retention/)
- **Watch time matters:** in the MrBeast memo, the video with the higher average view duration (+1 min 38 s) got about **3x the views** (120M vs 45M). The metrics to track are CTR, AVD and AVP. — [Alexander Jarvis / MrBeast memo](https://www.alexanderjarvis.com/memo-how-to-succeed-in-mrbeast-production/)

### Inferences
- **Starting targets for a new channel with 8-10 minute videos:**
  - Intro (30 s) of **≥65-70%**.
  - Average view percentage (AVP) of **35-50%**. This is a reasonable inference from the sources, not an official benchmark.
  - After 5-10 videos, switch to comparing against the channel's own "typical retention".

### Gaps
- No public, verifiable benchmarks specific to football or history documentaries, or to Spanish-language channels.
