# Packaging (títulos/miniaturas/CTR) y copyright/IA para documentales de historia del fútbol en YouTube (2025-2026)

Context: Spanish-language channel "LOS 11" (Argentina), ElevenLabs AI narration, first video = Istanbul 2005 UCL final (UEFA footage). Research date: 2026-11-02.

## Titles: length, curiosity vs clarity, examples, Test & Compare status

### Takeaway
Title testing is now official. YouTube's own help page says Test & Compare covers "Title only, Thumbnail only, or Title and thumbnail", with up to 3 variants, and picks the winner by watch time share, not CTR. A title has to win the click and also deliver on its promise. I found no dataset of high-performing Spanish-language football-history titles, so the title patterns below are inferences, not measured results.

### Cited Findings
- Test & Compare lets you test "Title only, Thumbnail only, or Title and thumbnail", with up to 3 variants. It is desktop only (YouTube Studio) and needs advanced features turned on. It does not support Shorts, scheduled lives or Premieres (a Premiere qualifies once it becomes a regular long-form video). It can't be used on made-for-kids, mature-audience or private videos. — [YouTube Help: Test & Compare](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)
- The winner is the variant "with the highest watch time". Possible results are Winner, Performed Same and Inconclusive. With no clear winner, YouTube shows the first variant you uploaded. A test "should be completed within two weeks". — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13861714?hl=en)
- Timeline: thumbnail testing reached all creators with advanced features in 2024. Title testing started as an experiment and was expanded in July 2025. Some 2025 outlets claimed a full rollout and others said it was limited. The current help page now lists title testing as standard. — [Tubefilter, Jul 16 2025](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/07/16/youtube-feature-test-and-compare-titles-thumbnails/); [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-expand-test-compare-how-to-use-it/); [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/12/youtube-creators-can-test-multiple-video-thumbnails)
- YouTube Creator Liaison Rene Ritchie said the tool reports watch time instead of separate CTR and retention numbers because "watch time includes both!" — quoted in [Search Engine Journal, Dec 22 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/)
- Paddy Galloway (consultant for MrBeast and others): "If they don't click, they don't watch." He puts the psychology of the click at about 80-90% of a thumbnail's success and the design at only 10-20%, and recommends spending about 30% of production time on the idea, title and thumbnail. His idea filter checks audience fit, thumbnail simplicity, novelty and format stacking. These claims come from secondhand summaries of 2023-2025 interviews. — [Colin & Samir "New Rules of YouTube 2025" transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/new-rules-youtube-2025/); [Creator Science #152 summary](https://www.everand.com/podcast/704625165/GREATEST-HITS-152-Paddy-Galloway-The-most-sought-after-YouTube-consultant-on-the-planet-Clients-include-MrBeast-Jesser-Ryan-Trahan-and-Noah); [Medium interview recap](https://medium.com/@ammarsheikhs/meet-the-godfather-of-youtube-strategy-paddy-galloway-interview-fcfc652bec2c)
- News hook: Netflix has a documentary on the Istanbul 2005 comeback (Spanish title "Al descubierto en el Reino Unido: El Liverpool y el milagro de Estambul", listed as a 2026 film). The release date in the sources was unclear. — [Common Sense Media ES](https://www.commonsensemedia.org/es/resenas-pelicula/al-descubierto-en-el-reino-unido-el-liverpool-y-el-milagro-de-estambul); [kchcomunicacion](https://kchcomunicacion.com/?p=406639)
- In Spanish-language media the established name for the match is "el milagro de Estambul" (UEFA.com ES and LFC.com ES both use it). — [UEFA.com](https://www.uefa.com/uefachampionsleague/news/025d-0f7de386ee14-56c9c7d246e4-1000--el-milagro-de-estambul-la-victoria-del-liverpool-en-la-fin/); [LiverpoolFC.com ES](https://www.liverpoolfc.com/es/news/day-2005-liverpools-champions-league-miracle-istanbul)

### Inferences
- Because Test & Compare optimizes for watch time, a clickbait title that wins the click but loses viewers will lose the test. Test titles that make an honest promise.
- Proposed rules (not validated by data): keep the core idea within about 50-60 characters so it isn't cut off on mobile. Put the strongest search term near the front ("Estambul 2005", "Liverpool", "Milán"). Combine a clear subject with one open question or tension. Don't repeat the thumbnail text in the title; the two should complement each other.
- Example variants to A/B test for video #1:
  - (a) "El Milagro de Estambul: cómo el Liverpool remontó un 3-0 al Milan" (clear, searchable)
  - (b) "3-0 abajo en el descanso. Lo que pasó en el vestuario del Liverpool" (curiosity gap)
  - (c) "La final más loca de la historia de la Champions" (superlative)
- Use the Netflix doc's interest for search traffic, without implying any affiliation.

### Gaps
- I found no reliable published dataset of top-performing football-history titles in Spanish or English (for example from channels like Tifo Football, Hand of Pod or Spanish-language history channels). No sourced examples are available.
- I found no official YouTube or vidIQ data on optimal title length for 2025-2026. Character-count advice is common practice, not a sourced finding.

## Thumbnails: composition, faces, text, contrast, CTR benchmarks

### Takeaway
In 2025-2026 data, breakout thumbnails usually show one clear face (often filling about a third of the frame) or a high-contrast subject, with 0-5 words of text. Faces are common but don't help in every case: a 300K-video dataset found little overall lift from faces. Organic CTR of roughly 2-10% is normal, and the best benchmark is the channel's own 90-day average.

### Cited Findings
- vidIQ breakout thumbnail study (500 breakouts, 30 niches including sports and history, published June 30 and updated July 8, 2026):
  - 69% show a human face, rising to 80% in the top 50.
  - 89% have a face or high contrast.
  - Only about 5% use exaggerated expressions; a "clear, well-lit face with a genuine expression" is more common.
  - 72% have overlay text, with a median of 5 words. 28% have no text.
  - High contrast appears in 56% overall but only 46% of the top 50.
  - Top-50 layout: a face filling about 1/3 of the frame, strong background contrast, very few words.
  - Correlational only; no causal claims.
  — [vidIQ Research](https://vidiq.com/research/youtube-thumbnail-study/)
- 1of10 Media analysis of 300K+ viral 2025 videos: thumbnails with and without faces perform similarly overall. Faces helped only above some channel size and only modestly. Faces helped in finance and hurt in business. Multiple faces did better than a single face. — [Search Engine Journal, Dec 2025](https://www.searchenginejournal.com/do-faces-help-youtube-thumbnails-heres-what-the-data-says/563944/)
- Eden analysis of 507,478 videos: thumbnail text averages about 7 words. Breakout videos had slightly more 4-6 word text, and returns stop at around 7 words. — [Eden](https://eden.so/blog/what-makes-a-good-youtube-thumbnail/)
- Organic CTR range is roughly 2-10%, with 4-6% considered strong. Sources advise comparing against your own 90-day average rather than a universal benchmark. The often-cited 0.65% figure is for ads, not organic traffic. — [Focus Digital 2025](https://focus-digital.co/average-youtube-ctr-organic-paid-benchmarks-2025/); [vidIQ blog](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/6-tips-good-CTR-youtube); [teleprompter.com](https://teleprompter.com/blog/whats-a-good-click-through-rate-on-youtube)
- Vendor claims such as "faces = +38% CTR" or "6.8% vs 4.1%" have unverified methods. I treat them as low-reliability. — [miraflow](https://miraflow.ai/blog/youtube-ctr-benchmarks-2026)

### Inferences
- For football history, a player's face showing raw emotion (Gerrard pointing or shouting, Dudek's save, a despairing Maldini or Shevchenko) can serve as the face element.
- Problem: those faces are usually in Getty/AP/Reuters photos or UEFA broadcast frames, which are copyrighted (see the copyright section). Safer options:
  - Commissioned or AI illustrations (which need the disclosure check below if photorealistic).
  - Stylized artwork of the players.
  - CC-licensed Commons photos.
  - Text and scoreboard graphics, for example a huge "3-0" crossed out and turned into "3-3".
- A scoreboard-led thumbnail ("0-3 → 3-3") fits the "high contrast, no face" pattern used by 11% of breakouts, and it avoids copyright exposure.
- Keep overlay text at 0-5 words. Avoid YouTube's own red/white/black interface colours. Test 2-3 thumbnails with Test & Compare from day one.

### Gaps
- No niche-specific (sports/history) CTR benchmarks were found. vidIQ's sample includes those niches but doesn't break them out.
- No public CTR data for Spanish-language football channels.

## Copyright: match footage (UEFA/PL/FIFA/Serie A), Content ID, fair use / derecho de cita, monetization

### Takeaway
UEFA and other league or broadcaster footage is in Content ID. Matching clips get claims automatically, often set to block or monetize-for-claimant, and "a few seconds" is no safe harbour. Fair use is a valid reason to dispute, but the claimant decides, and pushing past a rejected appeal leads toward a takedown and a copyright strike. Argentina's derecho de cita (Ley 11.723 art. 10) is narrow, word- and bar-based, and contested for audiovisual material. It gives little practical protection. Avoid or minimize broadcast footage, and build the video from narration, graphics, maps, recreations and licensed or CC images.

### Cited Findings
- **Content ID disputes:**
  - Valid grounds: you hold the rights; a copyright exception "such as fair use"; or misidentification.
  - Not valid: giving credit, owning a copy, or not monetizing.
  - Claimant response time: 30 days for a dispute, 7 days for an appeal.
  - If the appeal is rejected, the claimant "could then submit a copyright removal request". If valid, "your video would be removed... and your channel would get a copyright strike". A counter-notification forces the claimant to sue.
  - "Repeated or malicious abuse of the dispute process can result in penalties."
  — [YouTube Help: Dispute a Content ID claim](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797454?hl=en)
- **YouTube's fair use page:**
  - Content ID can't decide fair use; courts do.
  - Credit and disclaimers don't create fair use: "There aren't any magic words".
  - Short use isn't automatically safe: taking the "heart" of a work can be unfair even if brief, and claims can block monetization "even if you only use a few seconds".
  - In the EU, exceptions are limited to "quotation, criticism, review, caricature, parody, and pastiche".
  — [YouTube Help: Fair use](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148?hl=en)
- **Reused-content policy (monetization, separate from copyright):**
  - Allowed: "Edited footage... where you add a storyline and commentary" and "Using clips for a critical review".
  - Not allowed: "Clips of moments... edited together with little or no narrative".
  — [YouTube YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- **UEFA and Content ID (older reports, 2017-2018):**
  - UEFA says it takes "the unauthorised use of its footage extremely seriously". — [UEFA.com](https://www.uefa.com/news-media/news/026b-12a6b2df73c1-a95682a7cd89-1000--uefa-committed-to-tackling-audiovisual-piracy-during-uefa)
  - In 2017, a Slovenian TV show using UEFA clips was blocked with a "blocked by UEFA" copyright message. UEFA said such videos "are automatically blocked by YouTube without UEFA's intervention", which shows the matching is automated and the action is set by policy. — [Josimar (c. 2017)](https://josimar.no/artikler/criticism-of-ceferin-silenced-again)
  - A 2018 viral World Cup remix was blocked via Content ID even though rights groups considered it fair use or fair dealing. — [EDRi](https://edri.org/our-work/uefa-1-right-remix-0-viral-video-gets-blocked/)
- **Short clips and UK law:** UK courts have held that even clips of about 8 seconds showing key moments can be a "substantial part" of a broadcast. — reported in search results; probably refers to ECB v Tixdaq (2016), not verified by fetch. [Brodies](https://brodies.com/insights/media-broadcasting-and-sports/an-own-goal-for-uefa/)
- **Premier League history:** the Premier League sued YouTube in 2007 over clips. The case was thrown out or settled (2010-2013). It shows long-standing strict enforcement. — [SportBusiness](https://sportbusiness.com/news/premier-leagues-case-against-youtube-thrown-out-of-court); [SportBusiness settle](https://media.sportbusiness.com/news/premier-league-settles-youtube-copyright-case/)
- **US fair use for soccer clips (Public Citizen guide):** non-commercial, commentary-type uses are weighed more favourably. Monetized channels are commercial. — [Public Citizen](https://www.citizen.org/article/a-guide-to-fair-use-in-posting-soccer-video-clips/)
- **Argentina, Ley 11.723 art. 10:**
  - Allows "con fines didácticos o científicos, comentarios, críticas o notas" quoting up to 1,000 words of literary or scientific works or 8 bars of music. It has no audiovisual measure.
  - A Buenos Aires criminal chamber (Sala VII) said art. 10 does not clearly cover film. It still dismissed a case over a 9-second excerpt in a news programme because the clip was brief and informative.
  - Julio Raffo argues that applying art. 10 to online video rests on "a misreading" of the article.
  — [Otros Cines (Raffo)](https://www.otroscines.com/nota-15191-legislacion-cinematografica-v-el-derecho-de-cita-en-la); [CERLALC jurisprudencia](https://cerlalc.org/wp-content/uploads/dar/jurisprudencia/1427.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Practical rule set for the Istanbul video:**
  1. Assume any UEFA broadcast footage, even 3-5 seconds, can match Content ID. Possible outcomes: revenue goes to the claimant, the video is blocked in some territories or worldwide, or (worst case) a manual takedown and strike.
  2. If you use footage, keep each clip very short, put narration or analysis over it (not raw match audio or commentary), and space clips out. Upload as **unlisted** first, wait for the copyright check in Studio (Checks), then trim or mute flagged segments with Studio's editor before publishing.
  3. Never dispute just because "I gave credit" or "it's not monetized". Dispute on fair use only for genuinely transformative, short, commented clips, and remember that a rejected appeal can become a strike. With a strike-free new channel, accept monetization claims rather than escalate.
  4. Derecho de cita (Argentina) and EU "quotation" give weak protection for match footage. YouTube enforces globally through Content ID anyway, so legal theory rarely matters in practice.
  5. Footage-light production (narration, animated tactics boards, maps, timelines, newspaper headlines, stadium b-roll you own, CC photos) is the most monetization-safe approach, and it fits the "storyline and commentary" test of the reused-content policy.
- **Photos:**
  - Getty/AP/Reuters/AFP match photos are commercially licensed. Using them without a license is infringement regardless of credit. Agencies enforce through letters and invoices rather than Content ID (inferred; no 2025 source found).
  - Wikimedia Commons files are usable if you follow each file's license:
    - CC BY / CC BY-SA: credit using TASL (Title, Author, Source link, License and version), in the description and/or the end credits.
    - CC BY-SA adaptations can trigger share-alike on the adapted image.
    - Check each file's page individually; some are uploaded with wrong licenses.
  — TASL guidance: [uOttawa library](https://www.uottawa.ca/library/copyright/what-is-copyright/how-attribute-creative-commons-licensed-content-best-practices); [Smartcopying](https://smartcopying.edu.au/how-to-attribute-creative-commons-licensed-materials/)
- **Music:** match broadcasts often carry the UEFA Champions League anthem. Its audio alone can trigger claims, so mute broadcast audio.

### Gaps
- I found no 2024-2026 first-hand reports or Reddit threads quantifying UEFA's current Content ID policy (block vs monetize, per territory) for historical UCL finals. Also nothing for FIFA, Serie A or the Premier League on history channels. Outcomes vary by claimant (UEFA vs the broadcaster: BT/TNT, Sky, Mediaset for 2005, ESPN/Fox Latin America).
- No official "safe clip length" exists. YouTube explicitly says there are no magic rules.
- No 2025 source on Getty's enforcement against YouTube creators specifically.
- No ruling found on YouTube video under Argentine art. 10.

## AI voice / synthetic content disclosure and the 2025 "inauthentic content" monetization policy

### Takeaway
On July 15, 2025, YouTube renamed "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". YouTube describes it as a clarification, not a new rule. It targets mass-produced, template-based or barely varied videos regardless of whether AI was used. An ElevenLabs-narrated channel stays monetizable if each video is original, researched storytelling with real editorial value. Disclosure of altered or synthetic content is required only for realistic content that could mislead: a real person saying or doing something they didn't, altered footage of real events, or realistic fake scenes. A generic AI narrator voice isn't explicitly listed as requiring disclosure.

### Cited Findings
- The July 15, 2025 update renamed the policy from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content". It covers content that is "mass-produced or repetitive", and the page lists "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" as ineligible. It also lists "Content that exclusively features readings of other materials you did not originally create". Using AI "to edit your video scripts or generate a unique background visual" can be acceptable if the final product shows creative vision and educational or entertainment value. — [YouTube YPP policies](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- YouTube called it a "minor update" to a long-standing guideline. It applies regardless of whether AI is used. Examples given: channels with narrative stories that differ only superficially, and slideshows that all share the same narration. — [Social Samosa](https://www.socialsamosa.com/news-2/youtube-clarifies-monetisation-policy-targeting-inauthentic-content-9493155); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/no-new-rules-just-clearer-language-youtube-updates-monetisation-guidelines-74549.htm)
- Some outlets overstated it ("YouTube stops paying creators of AI slop"; "videos without human contribution not eligible"). — [EWN](https://www.ewn.co.za/2025/07/14/youtube-stops-paying-creators-of-ai-slop); compare [Typecast](https://typecast.ai/learn/youtube-ai-monetization-july-15-ypp-update/)
- **Altered or synthetic content disclosure** is required for realistic content that:
  - "Makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do"
  - "Alters footage of a real event or place"
  - "Generates a realistic scene that didn't actually occur"
  - Creates AI music that is the focus of the video.

  Exempt: unrealistic or animated content, minor edits, AI help with "outlines, scripts, titles, thumbnails, or infographics", captions, upscaling, and cloning "one's own voice to create voice overs or dubs". Creators who consistently fail to disclose "may be subject to manual application of a label, or penalties", including removal or YPP suspension. — [YouTube Help: altered/synthetic content](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)

### Inferences
- **ElevenLabs narration:**
  - A stock or designed AI voice used as a narrator isn't impersonating a real person, so it likely doesn't require the label. Cloning the creator's own voice is explicitly exempt.
  - Cloning a real commentator's or player's voice (e.g. AI "Gerrard" or "Benítez" speaking lines they never said) **requires** disclosure and carries legal and personality-rights risk. Avoid it.
- **Photorealistic recreations need the label:** AI images or video realistic enough to pass as the 2005 match (a fake "photo" of the half-time dressing room) count as a "realistic scene that didn't occur". Stylized or illustrated recreations don't. When in doubt, tick the box; the label has no stated monetization penalty, but non-disclosure does.
- **Staying clear of the inauthentic-content policy:**
  - Original research script with a distinct authorial voice (sources, interviews, analysis).
  - Varied visual structure per video, not one template reused with only names swapped.
  - Don't publish at high-volume "farm" cadence.
  - Optionally add the creator's own on-camera or voice presence, such as an intro or human-voiced segments.
  - Channel-level review at YPP application looks at the overall pattern, so the first 10-20 videos should all clearly show original storytelling.
- Combining heavy match-footage reuse with AI narration raises both reused-content and inauthentic-content risk. Footage-light, commentary-heavy videos reduce both.

### Gaps
- YouTube's official pages don't explicitly say whether a non-cloned synthetic narrator voice (TTS) on its own needs disclosure. The inference above is based on the listed categories.
- No public data on how often AI-narrated documentary channels were demonetized after July 2025. Reports are anecdotal or vendor-written (Fliki, Typecast have a commercial interest).
